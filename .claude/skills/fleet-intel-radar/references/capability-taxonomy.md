# Capability taxonomy and classification labels

Assess the market from the operator's side: the jobs a fleet operator needs to get done, not how many features a vendor lists. If you add or rename a capability, record it in `methodology.md` with the date.

## Capability list

Each domain is a group. Pick the capabilities that are visible in the sources, and add rows when a new one appears.

1. **Tracking and core telematics:** live location and trip history, geofencing, hardware options, plug-and-play and OEM or embedded data, vehicle and asset coverage.
2. **Driver safety and behaviour:** driver scoring, coaching workflows, in-cab alerts, risk analytics, driver engagement.
3. **Video and computer vision:** dashcam and video telematics, AI event detection, driver-facing cameras, incident review and evidence.
4. **Maintenance and diagnostics:** fault codes and diagnostics, service scheduling, predictive maintenance, tyre condition and pressure, defect reporting.
5. **Fuel, EV and sustainability:** fuel and idling analytics, EV suitability and transition planning, charging, emissions and CO2 reporting.
6. **Compliance and regulatory:** tachograph and driver hours, daily vehicle checks, licence checks, regional reporting requirements.
7. **Operations and workflow:** job dispatch, routing and ETAs, proof of delivery, forms, workflow orchestration.
8. **Analytics and intelligence:** dashboards and reporting, benchmarking, predictive analytics, prescriptive recommendations.
9. **AI assistants and automation:** natural-language querying, copilots, agentic workflows, automated actions, human-in-the-loop controls, explainability and grounding.
10. **Data, integrations and ecosystem:** open APIs, marketplace or partner ecosystem, OEM data integration, TMS and ERP links, data export and portability.
11. **Commercial and platform:** pricing and packaging as disclosed, onboarding, security and data certifications, multi-region support.
12. **Segment fit:** vans and light commercial, HGV, cars, mixed fleets, regulated sectors.

## Status labels (for every cell in the capability matrix)

- **Publicly verified:** a dated, public, primary source shows it working or generally available.
- **Partially verified:** some evidence, but scope, availability or depth is unclear, or only secondary sources.
- **Announced or planned:** stated as coming. No evidence it is available.
- **Not publicly verified:** nothing public found. This is **not** a statement that the capability is missing.
- **Not applicable:** outside what the organisation does.

Each cell records: status, evidence ID, source date, confidence (High, Medium, Low).

## Relative position (separate from status)

Add only when the evidence supports a comparison.
- **Leading:** clearly deeper or earlier than most of the group, on evidence.
- **Competitive:** comparable to most of the group.
- **Lagging:** clearly behind, on evidence. Not the same as "not publicly verified".
- **Unclear:** the evidence doesn't support a call.

## AI evidence ladder

Label every AI item with exactly one:
1. **Marketing claim:** said in promotional material, with no product detail.
2. **Announced capability:** the vendor says it is built or coming, with specifics.
3. **Demonstrated functionality:** shown working in a demo, a recording, documentation or release notes.
4. **Generally available:** customers can buy or turn it on now.
5. **Adoption with realised value:** evidence of customers using it and a measured result.

## AI progression

For each organisation, place it on the highest step with evidence:
**Descriptive analytics → Predictive intelligence → Recommended actions → Automated execution.**
Say what the evidence is. If the evidence stops at the ladder's "announced" level, say so.

## Radar maturity (AI and innovation radar)

- **Emerging:** early signals, a few vendors, mostly announced or demonstrated.
- **Experimenting:** several vendors have it live in pilots, previews or limited release.
- **Scaling:** generally available at multiple vendors, with customer adoption visible.
- **Mainstream:** expected by customers, offered by most of the primary group.

Each radar entry names the capability, the level, the evidence behind the level, the vendors involved, and what would move it up or down.
