The Silent Four

Four responders went almost quiet after 12 August. This file shows who, how the scoring rules let it happen, and what to tell a developer so a missed call can never push a hero out of the rotation.

Spec vs reality

Built as asked?

Ping wait: 90 to 60 secondsBuilt exactly

The askWiki brief: "Tune how long a ping waits for an answer before it moves on." No target, no reason, no check on what it does to scores.

What shippedIn the data, a missed ping moves on after 91 to 93 seconds before 12 Aug and 61 to 63 seconds after. The setting does what it says.

The catchThe glossary says a miss lowers a responder's place in the order. Nobody connected a shorter wait to more misses to lower scores.

Proximity weighted upBuilt exactly

The askWiki brief: "Weight proximity more heavily when ranking who gets pinged." Release notes say responders who cover wide areas asked for it.

What shipped0.45 to 0.60 on proximity, 0.40 to 0.25 on recent acceptance.

Open questionOn 14 Aug the engineering manager asked whether this was meant to apply to responders who turn jobs down. The staff engineer was away until the 24th and Priya left on the 21st. It was never answered, so the answer is "whatever the code does".

Did wide-area responders gain?Not visible

TestIf proximity counts for more, the home responder should be the first ping more often.

ResultIn the 11 areas without a silent responder, the home share of first pings fell from 80% to 72%. In the four affected areas it fell from 81% to 41%. I can't see ranking scores, so I can't isolate the proximity change. What the data shows is the opposite of the hoped-for effect.

How to know it worked

Replay the last 70 days of pings through the new rules. No responder should end up with a fraction of their peers' pings, and callouts nobody took should return to about 5%. If the availability record gets a new field, tell the Supply team first.

Plain-text brief

What I could not see

  • Real score history per responder. The replay is an estimate that starts everyone at 0.5.
  • How long people take to say yes. The cascade ends when someone accepts, so only refusals and misses leave a timing trail.
  • The working code for availability, travel time and phone push. Those functions are blank placeholders in the routing folder.
  • Who was available on callouts where only one responder was pinged.
  • Prior-year numbers and anything after 6 September.
