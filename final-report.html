On 12 August Release 4.2 shipped with three changes. From that morning, responders missed far more offers, and four of them nearly stopped hearing from us. This is what happened, how I know, what I'd ship to fix it, and the check that keeps the next brief honest.

The plain version

Offers began timing out, and one group of responders couldn't climb back.

12 Aug · 09:04

4.2 ships. First missed ping that morning. Missed pings that day: 28%, against 0–5% a day before.

12 Aug · 15:41

First “offer vanished” ticket, seven hours after the first miss.

14–15 Aug

Four responders' pings stop: Farlight, The Undertow, Vesper, Meteor Mite.

First “phone's gone quiet” ticket. Ticket volume goes from 5–8 a week to 20–32.

6 Sep

Last data. Taken is back to 73%, misses still 5× the old rate.

1 · Change

4.2 shortened the wait for an answer from 90s to 60s, shifted ranking toward proximity, and made console filters stick. The two routing briefs were one sentence each, with no goal.

2 · Effect

Misses rose in all 16 responders, all 15 areas, every time of day. Callouts nobody took doubled, 5.5% to 11.1%. Refusals held roughly steady (21% → 18%).

3 · Trap

In the code a miss costs the same as a “no” (−0.12), only a yes gives points back (+0.08), and scores never fade. Four responders slid and stayed there.

Sources: pings, support_tickets, 00-rook/4-2-impact-analysis.md, history.py and config.py. Priya's view was “mostly seasonal, back in September”. The data doesn't support that (slide 6).

The mechanism (from code)

Every miss costs more than a yes earns, and nothing ever resets.

# history.py · config.py record_accepted: score + 0.08 record_declined: score − 0.12 "…or we ran out of time waiting. Same either way." # TODO(2019): ease back toward neutral? # "Leaving it as-is for now."
  • Start at 0.5 (neutral), floor 0, ceiling 1.
  • Break-even failure rate is 40%: above it, the score drifts down.
  • Average failure went from ~23% to ~36%, so ordinary responders are close to the edge.
  • From 0 it takes ~7 yeses in a row to reach neutral.

What's proven, what isn't

Counts are solid. Causes are not.

Confidence 0–95, my scoring in 4-2-impact-analysis.md.

I changed my mind on the timeout

In Module 1 I read the timeout cut (90s → 60s) as “very likely the main mechanism”. In Module 3 I dropped it from 70 to 40: no refusal ever took longer than 40 seconds, before or after, so a quick “no” didn't need more time. A slow “yes” still might. Nobody has seen yes-answer times.

Seasonality is a weak fit

Six flat weeks come before, the step starts at 09:04 on release day, and a 15% dip in demand should lower ping counts, not raise the missed share. No prior-year data to test it directly.

Missing and who has it: yes-answer times and push logs (engineering manager), real scores and ranks (staff engineer), anything after 6 Sep (engineering manager).

The fix, as a prototype

Make going quiet visible, and give the handler one action.

  1. In Kip's console a Quiet banner marks Meteor Mite: 4 pings this week against 11 usual.
  2. Click Check in (or the banner's Review). Tick that you've confirmed they're available.
  3. Confirm. Standing resets to neutral, and a line lands in the override log.
  4. Switch to Meteor Mite's phone: “You're still on the list.”

What the brief changes

  • A miss costs less than a refusal; scores ease back toward neutral, never above.
  • Handler sees Quiet with evidence, plus Check in.
  • Responder is told they're still on the list.

It doesn't promise offers, touch the 60s wait or proximity weights, or fix the wider miss spike. That's about one third of the fall.

Your prototype, 05-super-speed/prototype.html, running here unchanged. Meteor Mite's ping drop is real (to 6 Sep). “Last yes”, the last three outcomes, the Quiet trigger and the banner are placeholders. Nothing here changes a real score.

Pitch to a responder: “You're still on the list, and we'll tell you what happened to your last few offers.” Before anyone builds: see the real scores first. If the four aren't at the floor, the brief changes. Full brief.

Keeping it from happening again

review-checklist: four questions before any brief ships.

The 4.2 routing briefs were one sentence each. My skill reads a brief and returns the same verdict every time: owner, success measure, consistent scope, problem before fix.

What it does and doesn't do

  • Does: force a goal, a measure and an owner onto the page before build. A one-line brief fails three of four tests.
  • Doesn't: know the system. It would not have found the penalty loop; it makes someone write down how a shorter wait feeds into scores.
  • Prints in chat; edits nothing unless asked.

The habit around it

  • A Monday review reminder runs it over every brief. Next run 26 Oct, ~9:43. Output goes to 06-sidekicks/scheduled-run-output.txt.
  • renew-reminders renews it the day before expiry (25 Oct).
  • Caveat both are session-only. They fire only while the session is open and die if it closes.

The 4.2 verdicts apply the skill's own rules to the one-sentence wiki briefs (“Weight proximity more heavily when ranking”, “Tune how long a ping waits”). They are not a recorded run. The Module 5 verdict is a real run on 05-super-speed/brief.md.
