Release 4.2: what happened, and how we stop a repeat.

A shorter wait on every offer, a penalty that never fades, and four responders who quietly stopped getting work. Here is the evidence, the exact prompts that found it, the fix I prototyped, and the skill that guards the next brief.

The short version

What happened, in four beats.

Data runs 29 Jun to 6 Sep 2026, so everything below is a picture of early September. Today is 2 Nov.

1 · 12 AUG
6 changes

4.2 shipped six changes at once. Two matter most: the wait for an answer went from 90s to 60s, and ranking leaned harder on distance.

2 · THE SPIKE
2.3% → 18%

Offers that nobody answered ("missed") jumped on release morning. Offers taken fell from 76.6% to 64.0%. Refusals barely moved.

3 · THE FOUR
3 of 40

Farlight, The Undertow, Vesper and Meteor Mite took 3 of 40 first pings after 4.2, against 175 of 247 before. They are about one third of the damage; the other twelve, about two thirds.

4 · THE ROOT
1 line

Both 4.2 briefs were one sentence each, with an owner (Priya) but no goal and no success measure. Engineering built them exactly as written, and nobody saw how they would interact.

Sources: pings table (database), 00-rook/4-2-impact-analysis.md, CLAUDE.md (Modules 1, 3 and 4). Counts are observed; the cause is not yet proven (slide 9).

X-Ray Vision · 11 prompts

One line takes points off. One line puts them back. Nothing fades.

My prompt#7 of 11
Find me the part of this code that takes points off somebody when they miss a ping or turn one down. Show it to me and explain it in plain English. Then find me every single thing in this code that puts points back on.

The answer: a "yes" adds 0.08; a "no" or a miss takes off 0.12 (history.py, config.py). No decay, no reset, no bonus. Prompt #11 then asked what the code does not do.

Inferred · replay of the code's rules, not real scores

Anyone who fails more than 40% of offers slides to the floor.

Expected drift per offer = 0.08 × (share taken) − 0.12 × (share failed), starting at 0.5 and held between 0 and 1. Average failure went from about 23% to about 36% after 4.2, which put ordinary responders about 4 points from the cliff. Real scores are not in the database.

How it doesn't happen again

Both 4.2 briefs are one sentence each, with an owner but no goal and no success measure. I ran review-checklist on their exact wiki text on 2 Nov. Verdict on both: Needs changes.

What keeps it running, for now

Honest limit. The tests read the document, not the facts or the interactions. Test 3 passed both 4.2 briefs, and the 2 Nov brief is "Ready" while the small fix's number is still unconfirmed.
Monday review. A session-only reminder runs it on every brief.md and appends the full output to 06-sidekicks/scheduled-run-output.txt. Next run 9 Nov. It covers only files named brief.md; briefs elsewhere, such as the wiki, are not checked.
Renewal. renew-reminders re-creates it before its 7-day expiry. Both die if the session closes. They already died once, between 20 Oct and 1 Nov, and were re-created.
How I'll know it works. Every brief has a recorded "Ready" run before anyone builds. Today: one brief on file, Ready on 2 Nov. The 4.2 briefs had no run before build. This is not yet tracked over time.
After launch. Nothing here watches for silence once a change ships (the first quiet ticket came on 17 Aug). The Quiet marker in the prototype (slide 10) would. It is not built.
